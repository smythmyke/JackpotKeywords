# I Tested Every Free Keyword Research Tool. Most of Them Are Lying to You.

*An honest 2026 ranking of the free tools that actually give you usable data — and the ones that quietly mislead you into bad decisions.*

There's a strange pattern in the keyword research industry. The tools that give you the most accurate data show you the least of it. The tools that show you everything you want to see are estimating most of it. And the marketing for both groups looks identical.

I spent a few weeks running the same set of test keywords through every free tier I could find — Google Keyword Planner, Ubersuggest, AnswerThePublic, Google Trends, JackpotKeywords, and a handful of "free forever" tools that turned out to want your credit card by step three. Same keywords, same niches. The numbers came back wildly different.

This is what I learned about which free tools are actually useful, what they trade off, and when free stops being free enough.

## What "free" actually means in this market

Every free keyword tool makes one of three tradeoffs:

1. **Real data, restricted view** — you get accurate numbers but limited or obscured (Google Keyword Planner's volume ranges fall here).
2. **Estimated data, unrestricted view** — you can run as many queries as you want, but the numbers are guesses (Ubersuggest's daily allowance is the canonical example).
3. **Real data, restricted volume** — you get full-fidelity numbers but only a few searches (JackpotKeywords' three free searches).

There is no fourth option. Anyone offering "unlimited real-time Google data for free forever" is either lying or about to be acquired and shut down.

Once you understand which tradeoff you're making, the rest of the comparison gets a lot easier.

## Which free tools give you real search volume?

Real volume — actual numbers from Google rather than estimates — is the most valuable single data point in keyword research. It's also the hardest to get for free.

### Google Keyword Planner

The only tool that gives you Google-sourced volume at no cost. The catch: without active ad spend, it shows ranges (1K–10K, 10K–100K) instead of exact numbers. A keyword in the 1K–10K range could be 1,200 searches or 9,800. That's an 8× spread on the metric you most need to be precise about.

Despite that, it's still the most reliable free data source if you can work around the ranges. The workaround most people don't know: a $1–5/day campaign unlocks exact data, even if the campaign barely runs.

### JackpotKeywords (3 free searches)

Connects to the same Google Ads API as Keyword Planner but returns *exact* monthly volumes, precise CPC ranges, competition levels, trend direction, and a composite score for each keyword. Each free search returns 1,000+ keywords organized into 12 intent categories. Three searches isn't unlimited, but it's enough to thoroughly research three products, niches, or campaigns. The data quality per search is the highest you'll find at zero cost.

### Ubersuggest (3 free searches per day)

Estimated volume and keyword difficulty. The data comes from clickstream panels, not Google directly, which means numbers can diverge from reality by 30–60%. Clean interface, beginner-friendly, generous daily allowance — but the accuracy tradeoff is real and worth knowing about. If you're making budget decisions, that 30–60% gap matters.

### Google Trends

No absolute volume numbers — just relative interest on a 0–100 scale. That sounds limiting until you realize it's perfect for two specific jobs: comparing two keywords against each other, and identifying seasonal patterns. Doesn't replace volume tools, but completes them.

When you need to research systematically across many topics, compare dozens of keywords confidently, or plan an ad budget with precision, the limitations start to bite.

## What's the best free tool for beginners?

If you're new to keyword research, the tool matters less than understanding what you're looking at. Volume, CPC, competition, and trends are the four metrics that drive most decisions. Any tool that surfaces at least two of them is useful.

For a first-ever keyword research session, **JackpotKeywords' free tier** is the most approachable because it eliminates the seed keyword problem. You describe your product or service in plain English — *"handmade soy candles sold on Etsy"* or *"project management software for remote teams"* — and the AI generates keywords across 12 intent categories. No need to know what seed keyword to start with, because there are no seed keywords.

**Google Keyword Planner** is the second-best beginner option, assuming you're comfortable creating a Google Ads account. The interface is built for advertisers and can feel overwhelming, but the Discover New Keywords feature is straightforward once you find it.

**Ubersuggest** offers the cleanest, most intuitive interface for beginners. Enter a keyword, see suggestions with metrics. The data accuracy concern is less important when you're learning the process than when you're making spend decisions.

**Google Trends** is the easiest tool to use but the hardest to act on alone. It tells you "cold brew coffee" peaks every summer and "hot cocoa" peaks every winter, but without volume numbers you can't decide whether either is worth targeting. Use it alongside a volume tool, not as your starting point.

## When does free stop being free enough?

Don't upgrade because paid tools feel "professional." Upgrade when specific limitations start costing you time or money:

**When volume ranges cost you time.** If you're spending 30+ minutes per session triangulating where keywords fall in Keyword Planner's 1K–10K bucket — checking Trends for relative comparison, scanning result counts on Google, eyeballing Autocomplete frequency — a $10/month tool that returns exact numbers saves more time than it costs.

**When you need to research more than three topics.** JackpotKeywords' free tier gives you three full searches. Each generates 1,000+ keywords, so three searches cover three products or niches comprehensively. Past that, the $9.99/month unlimited plan is the obvious next step.

**When you're planning real ad spend.** If you're about to invest $500+/month in Google Ads, making bid decisions on estimated data or volume ranges risks misallocating that budget. Exact volume and CPC data from a Google-sourced tool costs a fraction of the ad spend it protects.

**Don't upgrade just because a tool is popular.** SEMrush and Ahrefs are excellent — at $99–140/month they only make sense if you use their backlink analysis, rank tracking, and site audits *in addition to* keyword research. If keyword research is your primary need, a $10–30 tool delivers the same keyword data without the suite-pricing premium.
